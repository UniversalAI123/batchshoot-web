What BatchShoot does

BatchShoot turns a batch of raw product recordings into short, captioned videos. Everything happens on your iPhone: no account, and nothing is uploaded.

  1. Add recordings. Choose them from Photos or Files, record them in the app, or tap "Try a 15-second demo clip".
  2. Set your direction. One goal for the batch (tighten a take, a product demo, a buyer question answer, a short pitch, or captions with nothing cut) and one caption style.
  3. Generate edits. BatchShoot listens to each recording on your iPhone, writes the captions and proposes an edit that cuts pauses and likely restarts.
  4. Review. Tap a sentence to cut it or bring it back, fix a caption, and read the notes on lines worth checking before you post.
  5. Export MP4, then Save. The video goes to BatchShoot's Exports folder and the share sheet opens, so you can save it to Photos or send it on.

BatchShoot edits by what was said. It doesn't judge the picture, and every edit is yours to review before you export.

A batch holds up to 30 recordings, with a 1 GB total limit (2 GB when disk-backed exports are available). These limits keep an export inside what an iPhone's memory can hold. If a recording is over a limit, BatchShoot says which one before any work starts.

My recording is too long or too big

Trim it first, for example in Photos › Edit, then choose the trimmed copy.

My recording is 4K

BatchShoot on iPhone edits up to 1080p. Use a 1080p copy, or set Settings › Camera › Record Video to 1080p before you film.

Common questions

Do I need an account?

No. There's no sign-in. Open the app and add a recording, or try the demo clip.

Does listening need the internet?

Speech is processed on your iPhone. Builds using Apple’s on-device engine may need internet the first time to install English language assets. Older bundled-model builds need no model download. The demo comes with a prepared transcript; using it does not test speech-recognition accuracy.

Does BatchShoot upload my videos?

No. Listening, cutting, captions and export all run on your iPhone. See the privacy policy for the details.

Do I have to keep the app open?

Yes, while it listens and while it exports. iOS pauses apps in the background, so if you switch away or lock the phone, the work can stop. If it stops, come back and start the export again. Exports run one at a time.

Where do my finished videos go?

When an export finishes, tap Save. The video is written to Files › On My iPhone › BatchShoot › Exports, then the share sheet opens. Choose Save Video to put it in Photos, or AirDrop, Messages or any app that accepts video. If you close the share sheet, the file is still in the Exports folder. The batch export screen also offers "Save finished videos to Photos". It requests add-only Photos permission and confirms the number saved. Saving is separate from rendering; check the finished videos before posting.

Are the takes I record in the app saved to Photos?

No. A take recorded in BatchShoot stays inside the app until you export an edit. With "Remember this batch on this device" turned on, the batch is kept if the app closes; otherwise it lasts until you close the app.

Why do the colours look brighter than on my phone?

iPhones film in HDR by default, and an edit with captions, hook text, zoom, a mirror or a new frame can look brighter and more saturated than the original. BatchShoot tells you when a recording is HDR. Compare skin tones before you post. To keep the original colours, export with captions off, no hook text, the original frame, no mirror and no zoom. To film without HDR, turn off Settings › Camera › Record Video › HDR Video.

A caption misspells a name

Fix it once with "Fix a name across your batch", which changes it in every recording you select. For a product you film often, save a product profile with its spelling corrections and apply it to the next batch.

What does the claims check do?

It flags lines that often get product videos pulled or need a second look, such as medical claims, guarantees, earnings promises and price offers. It is a word list that runs on your iPhone, not any platform's rules and not legal advice. It never removes a line by itself; you decide.

The camera or microphone doesn't open

Allow them in iOS Settings › Apps › BatchShoot, then tap Record here again. You can always choose a recording from Photos or Files instead.

Your files

How do I delete a video?

Open Files › On My iPhone › BatchShoot › Exports and delete it. Copies you saved to Photos stay in Photos.

How do I delete everything?

Delete the BatchShoot app. That removes everything it stored, including the Exports folder, so save the videos you want to keep first. We have no copy and can't recover them.
